Mughal Garden collection · Prototype 01

Gulposh Anarkali

The embroidery for our first sample dress. Champagne-gold zardozi and dabka on ivory, with blush and sage resham filling the flowers. Every motif comes from a Mughal garden: the cusped mehrab arch, the poppy, the cypress and the jaal trellis.

Where the embroidery sits

Heaviest at the hem, lighter through the skirt, then a fine lattice on the bodice. This keeps the weight low so the kalis swing and hang straight. Hover over or tap a row to find it on the dress.

Sizes assume a 4.2 m hem, 16 kalis and 115 cm from empire waist to hem. Each kali is then about 185 mm wide at the top of the mehrab panel, which leaves a 12 mm margin each side. If the pattern changes, scale A and B by the same percentage.

Stitch key

How to read the drawings

Each technique is drawn the same way on every motif.

Dabka · coiled wire

All outlines, stems, the arch and lattice lines. Medium coil on A, B, E; fine (baarik) coil on C, D, F.

Nakshi · bright coil fill

Solid metal areas: poppy calyx, bud sepals, column capitals and plinths.

Resham · silk, long-and-short

Petals in gulabi blush, leaves and cypress in pista sage, rosettes in firozi mist.

Sitara · sequin, tacked centre

Rose-gold 3 mm sequins: stamen tips, cusp points, column chains and scatter.

Moti · pearl, 2–3 mm

Off-white pearls in rows along the arch, hem, neckline and cuffs; single moti at lattice nodes.

Sampling

Making the sample

  1. Stitch a test swatch first. Work panel A and 20 cm of border B on the final ivory organza before cutting the kalis.
  2. Transfer with the khaka. Print the khaka SVGs at 100% and check the 50 mm scale bar with a ruler. Then prick the outlines and pounce with chalk or neel.
  3. Back the organza on the adda. Tack fine cotton mulmul behind the kalis while embroidering, then trim it close afterwards. Organza alone will pucker under dabka.
  4. Weigh the swatch. Multiply up to estimate the hem weight. If it pulls the kalis off-grain, drop the cypress fill in B to outline only.
  5. Approve colour in daylight. Check the blush resham against the champagne dabka. The blush should look pink, not peach, next to the metal.
Your call

Decisions before we sample

  • Kali count and hem16 kalis and 4.2 m are assumed. More kalis means narrower panels, so A would need resizing.
  • Neckline shapeThe drawing is a U-neck. A sweetheart or boat neck would need its own yoke drawing.
  • PearlsFreshwater pearls or glass pearls. This changes the cost and the weight noticeably at about 3.6k pieces.
  • Back of the dressShould the back bodice carry the full jaal or a lighter buti, and do we want a back neck motif?
  • DupattaBorder B at 60% scale with buti D scattered would match. Should it join this prototype?
